What a binaural beat is

Play a 220 Hz tone in your left ear and a 226 Hz tone in your right, and most people hear a gentle pulse six times a second, though neither ear receives one. Wikipedia describes it as “an auditory illusion” that occurs when two slightly different tones are presented one to each ear (Beat (acoustics)). That's why headphones are needed: on speakers, the two tones mix in the air and you hear an ordinary beat, or nothing much.

In Hum & Bell the two tones sit an octave above the drone's root, quietly, under the rest of the sound. You can choose a 4, 6 or 10 Hz difference.

What the research says

Many websites claim that binaural beats change your brainwaves and, with them, your mood, focus or sleep. The evidence is weaker than those claims suggest. A 2023 systematic review of fourteen EEG studies found five with results in line with the “brainwave entrainment” idea, eight with contradictory results and one mixed, and noted how different the studies' methods were (Ingendoh, Posny and Heine, PLOS ONE, 2023).

So Hum & Bell offers the binaural tone as a sound you might enjoy, nothing more. If you like it, use it; if not, leave it off.

Using it comfortably

  • Use headphones, and keep the volume low. The tone should sit underneath the session, not on top of it.
  • Don't listen while driving or doing anything that needs your full attention.
  • If it's unpleasant in any way, turn it off. Nothing about the practice depends on it.
